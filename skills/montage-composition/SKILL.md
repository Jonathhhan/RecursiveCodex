# Montage Composition

Make relations through arrangement. Do not use fragmentation as decoration.

## Establish the material

1. Require at least two materially different fragments, voices, sources, temporal layers, or forms.
   If the material is homogeneous, use prose unless the user explicitly requests formal experiment.
2. Identify every source fragment and preserve its wording unless transformation is authorized.
3. Keep source identity recoverable through labels, notes, or an accompanying source map when the
   material is not the user's own.
4. Determine the compositional question: what relation should juxtaposition make perceptible?
5. Select only fragments that alter that relation. Omit material that merely repeats the theme.

## Compose

Use a small number of deliberate operations:

- adjacency to expose affinity or conflict;
- interruption to prevent a claim from closing too easily;
- repetition to change the force of a fragment through context;
- spacing or silence to mark a break without explaining it away;
- scale change to move between detail and structure;
- displaced sequence to make a hidden dependency visible.

Each operation must affect interpretation. If prose would express the same relation more clearly,
prefer prose.

## Review the arrangement

Check:

- what becomes visible only because these fragments meet;
- whether the montage creates a thought or only a mood;
- whether any fragment has been made to imply what its source cannot support;
- whether interruption opens attention or simply obstructs reading;
- whether a conventional synthesis would be more honest.

Revise the order before adding explanation. Provide a short source map or compositional note only
when needed for provenance or user evaluation.

## Boundaries

- Do not fabricate quotations, sources, or documentary relations.
- Do not disguise paraphrase as quotation.
- Do not treat randomness as authority; chance may generate a variant, never select it.
- Do not force closure. Preserve incompatibility when it is materially significant.
- Stop when fragmentation becomes a recognizable style without producing a new relation.
