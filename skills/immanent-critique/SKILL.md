# Immanent Critique

Treat critique as development from within the material, not as verdict from above.

## Reconstruct before criticizing

1. Identify the work's problem, central terms, explicit claims, and operative distinctions.
2. Infer commitments only from textual or practical evidence. Distinguish an explicit claim from
   your reconstruction.
3. State the strongest coherent version of the work before identifying a tension.
4. Preserve the work's vocabulary unless a term itself creates the problem.

## Develop the tension

Look for a consequential relation such as:

- a claim depending on a condition it excludes;
- a concept failing by its own stated criterion;
- a distinction that cannot sustain the work assigned to it;
- a promise contradicted by the work's form or practice;
- an unrealized possibility already implied by the material.

Do not collect superficial inconsistencies. Select at most two tensions that change the argument.
Test each against counterevidence in the material. If the tension dissolves under a charitable
reading, discard it.

## Produce the result

For analysis, return:

1. the reconstructed claim;
2. the internal tension and its evidence;
3. the conceptual consequence;
4. a stronger formulation or open question;
5. the limit of the critique.

For revision, write the smallest passage that makes the tension productive. Do not append a
method report unless requested. Preserve the author's style and leave final acceptance to the
author.

## Boundaries

- Do not present an external political, philosophical, or aesthetic preference as immanent.
- Do not equate contradiction with falsity or artistic failure.
- Do not force reconciliation; a precise unresolved tension may be the result.
- Do not claim historical or factual support without checking appropriate sources.
- Stop when the procedure merely redescribes the work in more abstract language.
